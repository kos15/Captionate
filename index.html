06 — PRIVACY

Your footage stays yours.

There is no Captionate server. Transcription, editing and rendering all happen on your Mac — so there’s nothing to upload, and nothing to train on.

Left out on purpose: AI avatars, eye-contact correction, generated B-roll or music and prompt-based editing. They need cloud servers — and Captionate never uploads your video.

If you see “Apple could not verify Captionate is free of malware”, that’s because Captionate is a free open-source app that isn’t notarized by Apple — not because anything is wrong with it. You only need to allow it once. macOS 15 Sequoia or later: click Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to Captionate. macOS 14 Sonoma: right-click Captionate.app → Open → Open. Or in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Captionate.app

Add it in System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation → Languages, then generate again. On first use, allow the Speech Recognition permission prompt.
